# GBP profile completeness now an explicit ranking input; review recency outweighs raw volume; Q&A replaced by AI-driven Ask Maps

**Date researched:** 2026-08-16
**Source:** [Local SEO Ranking Factors 2026: A Practical Guide for Multi-Location Brands](https://www.pinmeto.com/blog/local-seo-ranking-factors-2026/), PinMeTo

## Finding

Google's internal Google Business Profile (GBP) completeness score has become a more explicit ranking input in 2026: profiles missing service listings, photos, attributes, or Q&A content now face a measurable penalty relative to complete competitor profiles. The practical shift for 2026 is toward active, recent engagement rather than historical accumulation: fresh reviews, fast responses, current information, and a consistent posting rhythm now carry more weight, with review recency and response engagement mattering more relative to raw review volume than before. Google also tightened proximity signals in competitive categories, reducing the radius within which non-proximate businesses can rank for high-intent local queries. Separately, the GBP Q&A feature has been discontinued and replaced with "Ask Maps," an AI feature that answers visitor questions about a business in real time.

## Why it matters for SEO/AEO/GEO work

This is current, specific reinforcement of the `local-seo-gbp` sub-skill's guidance (from `SEO-PLAYBOOK.md` Section 22.1): profile completeness and review-response discipline are not just best practice, they are now measurably tied to ranking. It also means an older local-SEO checklist referencing "GBP Q&A" as a field to fill out needs updating, since Q&A has been replaced by Ask Maps; the actionable equivalent is making sure the business's own public content (website FAQ, schema, GBP description) contains the facts Ask Maps would need to answer accurately, since Ask Maps is itself another AI answer surface subject to the same machine-readability and freshness gatekeepers covered in the `aeo-geo-citations` sub-skill.
